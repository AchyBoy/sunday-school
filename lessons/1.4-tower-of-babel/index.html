Lesson 1.4: The Tower of Babel

Genesis 11:1-9 · Ages 14-17, about 30 kids · Lesson about 15 min + activity about 10 min
Idea menu, round 1. Every idea has a number. Tap ✎ on any of them: keep it, cut it, or tell me what to change. I'll build the final lesson from what you pick.

The passage

Genesis 11:1-9
1And the whole earth was of one language, and of one speech.
2And it came to pass, as they journeyed from the east, that they found a plain in the land of Shinar; and they dwelt there.
3And they said one to another, Go to, let us make brick, and burn them throughly. And they had brick for stone, and slime had they for morter.
4And they said, Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.
5And the LORD came down to see the city and the tower, which the children of men builded.
6And the LORD said, Behold, the people is one, and they have all one language; and this they begin to do: and now nothing will be restrained from them, which they have imagined to do.
7Go to, let us go down, and there confound their language, that they may not understand one another's speech.
8So the LORD scattered them abroad from thence upon the face of all the earth: and they left off to build the city.
9Therefore is the name of it called Babel; because the LORD did there confound the language of all the earth: and from thence did the LORD scatter them abroad upon the face of all the earth.

Pick ONE activity (about 10 min)

1. Silent tower build

Teams of 6 race to build the tallest tower out of cups or index cards and tape, 3 minutes. At 90 seconds every kid gets a secret card: only talk in questions · only Spanish · no talking, only humming · every sentence starts with "Banana" · disagree with the last person who spoke. The towers fall apart. The lesson shows itself.

Needs: about 150 cups or 5 packs of index cards + tape, 30 printed cards.

1b. Boys build a human pyramid, girls stack cups (your idea)

Same race, same clock. Boys: teams of 6 build a KNEELING pyramid, 3 on the bottom, 2, then 1 on top, and hold it for 10 seconds. Girls: teams race to stack a 36-cup pyramid and take it back down. Hand out the secret language cards at the start, so every team has to build it while they can't understand each other.

Safety rules for the pyramid: hands and knees only, nobody stands up, no more than 3 rows, on carpet or mats, shoes off, and 2 spotters at each pyramid. If the bottom row says down, everybody comes down. Ask the pastor first: some churches have rules about stunts like this.

Needs: about 150 plastic cups, a mat or carpet, a timer.

2. Skit: Babel Construction Co. (your idea)

I'm the foreman on the job site: "Bricks up, slime on, we're making a NAME for ourselves!" Halfway through, the workers draw cards and switch languages: auctioneer, Shakespeare, pirate, Cajun French, robot, sign language. A city inspector walks in at the end and shuts the job down.

Needs: 5-6 volunteers, the cards, about 5 min of script. Fits the 10 min with a short talk after.

3. Decoder race, one key split up (your idea)

Each team gets the key verse in a different code: symbols, Morse, emoji, pigpen, backwards. But each team only has PART of the key, so they have to go find the other teams and trade. No team finishes alone. That's Babel backwards.

Needs: 5 printed code sheets + key pieces. I make them.

4. Google Translate telephone

Before class I run Genesis 11:4 through 10 languages and back to English. Read the mangled version, they guess the verse. Short, quick laugh. Better as an opener than the main activity.

5. Back-to-back drawing

Pairs sit back to back. One describes a tower picture, the other draws it. No shape words allowed (no square, circle, triangle). Compare drawings. Everybody plays at once.

6. Name bricks

Every kid writes on a paper brick, no name: one thing people our age build to make a name for ourselves (followers, sports, grades, looks, being liked). Stack them into a tower on the table. Then knock it over, or leave it standing while I read Genesis 12:2. This could also be the object lesson INSIDE the lesson instead of the activity.

Pick the BIG POINT for the lesson (about 15 min)

7. Making a name for ourselves

Verse 4: "let us make us a name." One chapter later God tells Abram "I will make thy name great." At Babel they built their own name. With Abram, God gave it. Where do we try to build our own?

Genesis 12:2
2And I will make of thee a great nation, and I will bless thee, and make thy name great; and thou shalt be a blessing:

8. God came down to see it

Verse 5: they built a tower to reach heaven, and God still had to come down to see it. However big we build, it's small to Him.

9. Babel turned around: Pentecost

At Babel, one language was split into many and people were scattered. At Pentecost, many languages heard one message and people were gathered. It ends in Revelation with every language together before the throne.

Acts 2:6
6Now when this was noised abroad, the multitude came together, and were confounded, because that every man heard them speak in his own language.
Revelation 7:9
9After this I beheld, and, lo, a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues, stood before the throne, and before the Lamb, clothed with white robes, and palms in their hands;

10. Together is not always good

They were united, one language, and working hard. But they were united against what God said (fill the earth, Genesis 9:1). A group that's all on the same page can still be on the wrong page. Fits teens and friend groups.

Extras (add any)

Fun facts (pick 1 or 2)

11. Fun fact: bricks with a name stamped on them

⭐ Fun fact: They really did make a name on the bricks

In the Bible, Babel and Babylon are the same word. Centuries later, King Nebuchadnezzar (the one from Daniel) rebuilt Babylon and stamped HIS NAME into the bricks. Archaeologists have dug up thousands of them, and museums have them today.

Same city, same idea as verse 4, "let us make us a name." And Nebuchadnezzar said it out loud in Daniel 4:30: "Is not this great Babylon, that I have built... by the might of my power, and for the honour of my majesty?" The next verse, God humbled him.

Honest note: the stamped bricks are real, but they're from Nebuchadnezzar's time, long after the tower. Linking them to verse 4 is a connection, not something a verse says.

12. Fun fact: slime = tar, same stuff as Moses' basket

⭐ Fun fact: Slime for morter

Verse 3 "slime" is tar (bitumen) that bubbles up out of the ground in that part of the world. They had no stone, so they baked bricks and glued them with tar.

The same word shows up when Moses' mother made his basket: "daubed it with slime and with pitch" (Exodus 2:3). Same stuff: at Babel it built a tower for man's name, on the Nile it saved the baby God would use.

13. Kids who speak another language

If anybody speaks Spanish, Vietnamese, Cajun French (or has a grandparent who does), have them teach the class one phrase, like "God loves you."

14. Candy questions (2 or 3)

🍬 a. Why did they want to build the tower? Two reasons, both in verse 4.
To make a name, and so they wouldn't be scattered.
🍬 b. They built it to reach heaven. What did God have to do to see it?
Come down (verse 5).
🍬 c. What would you do? Your friend group is all in on something you know is wrong. Everybody agrees. What do you do?
EVERYONE who answers gets candy.

15. Closing: my story

A time I tried to make a name for myself, or a time I went along with the group. Tell me which, and I'll help word it.
