Lesson 1.5: The Tower of Babel

Genesis 11:1-9 · Series 1: In the Beginning · Ages 14-17, about 30 kids · About 29 minutes · Big idea: Just because you're busy building something doesn't mean you're building what God told you to build.
Written by Andrew Young · Book: God resists the proud and arrogant
Bring: 2 firm boards or tables (carpet) · 2 colors of plastic cups · the game timer on my phone (links page) · the rejection stickers · inspector's clipboard, pen, tape measure, fake nose and glasses · candy. Saturday: set up both boards and cups in the room and test the stacking. Before class: brief the inspector.
Who does what (3 ladies from the girls' class): Lady 1 = Building Inspector (her own sheet). Lady 2 = Boys' station helper. Lady 3 = Girls' station helper. They line up their team in threes and keep it moving at every buzzer. I run the timer, the lesson and the announcement.
SAY = how I might put it, not word for word · DO = action · ASK = question for them · ANSWER = the point I'm fishing for, let THEM find it · OPTIONAL = if there's time

Part 1. Noah, then a new group of builders 5 min

ASKAlright, who remembers what we talked about last lesson? What did Noah build?

ASKWho told him to build it?

ASKAfter the flood, did God give Noah and his family any instructions?

Genesis 9:1
1And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth.

SAYOkay, keep that in mind, because we're about to meet another group of builders.

Genesis 11:1-4
1And the whole earth was of one language, and of one speech.
2And it came to pass, as they journeyed from the east, that they found a plain in the land of Shinar; and they dwelt there.
3And they said one to another, Go to, let us make brick, and burn them throughly. And they had brick for stone, and slime had they for morter.
4And they said, Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.

ASKWhat are these people trying to build? Why do they say they want to build it?

ANSWER"Let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth."

🍬 1. What did God tell Noah's family to do? And what are these people trying to avoid doing?
Fill the earth (Genesis 9:1). They're trying NOT to be scattered. Let them connect the dots.
⭐ Fun fact: The oldest picture of Babylon's tower

Babel and Babylon are the same word in Hebrew, and the Bible puts both in the land of Shinar: Babel in Genesis 11:2 and 11:9, and Babylon's king carries the temple treasures "into the land of Shinar" in Daniel 1:2. Historians believe that about 600 BC, King Nebuchadnezzar (the king from Daniel) had a black stone carved with HIMSELF standing next to Babylon's giant temple tower. They believe it stood somewhere between 200 and 300 feet tall, and many believe it's tied to the Tower of Babel. In the museum's translation, he says he finished it "by raising its summit to the sky." Sound familiar? "whose top may reach unto heaven."

Hand a printed copy to each side. It's worn and hard to read; the printout says so.

Part 2. They were united 4 min

Genesis 11:5-6
5And the LORD came down to see the city and the tower, which the children of men builded.
6And the LORD said, Behold, the people is one, and they have all one language; and this they begin to do: and now nothing will be restrained from them, which they have imagined to do.
⭐ Fun fact: They built up, God came down

They built a tower "whose top may reach unto heaven." And verse 5 says "the LORD came down to see" it. Their biggest thing ever, and God still had to come down to look at it.

SAYOne language, one goal, everybody pulling the same way. And it was working. Working together is a good thing! God even says nothing would stop them. But look at what they teamed up to do: God said spread out, and they were working together to stay put.

ASKHave y'all ever been with a group of friends where everybody agreed to do something stupid?

OPTIONALIf nobody volunteers: "Can somebody just 'make something up'?" (big air quotes) "We know it really happened, but we're gonna pretend you're making it up."

SAYJust because everybody agrees doesn't mean everybody is right.

Don't read verses 7-9 yet. Save it for after the game.

SAYThese people had the materials, the manpower, and a plan. But how much of that depended on being able to understand each other? ...Well, we're about to find out.

Part 3. Babel Construction Company 10 min

SAYAlright, we're gonna play a little game. Boys against girls. It's called Babel Construction Company. Whoever has the tallest standing tower when time's up wins.

DOOne station each. One foreman + two builders per team, the rest line up in threes.

SAYNow, a few things you need to know. A foreman and two builders work at a time. Every 30 seconds the buzzer sounds, and you have 10 seconds to swap in the next three.

SAYOur official building inspector will be inspecting throughout construction. Every new foreman gets a rejection notice from her: what's wrong with your tower, and HOW you're allowed to talk until the next buzzer. Only the foreman reads it. Builders may not read rejection notices. At the end she'll do a final inspection and issue an official approval or rejection.

SAYAny questions? ...Good. Because I probably wasn't going to answer them anyway. Alright, is everyone ready? ...LET THE CONSTRUCTION BEGIN!

DOTimer: 2-minute game, switch every 30 seconds with a 10-second hands-off pause (screen goes red) while the next crew steps up. All one Start on my phone. First 30 seconds: normal talking. At every buzzer after that, the inspector hands the new foreman a REJECTED sticker. Only the foreman reads it. 2 minutes = 4 crews of 3, so run it twice if there's time and everybody gets a turn.

Only height wins. Her approvals and rejections don't count. Don't remind them until the end.

Part 4. Who won? Then back to Babel 6 min

DOTime's up, everybody stops. Inspector measures both towers.

ASKAlright, who won?

DOOffer a rematch, now that they know only the tallest wins.

ASKWait a minute. Is passing inspection how I said you win?

ASKThen why were y'all so worried about that inspector?

OPTIONALLet them explain themselves. Have fun with it.

SAYI told you what mattered at the very beginning. Then we started changing things. New foremen, new instructions, and this lady over here rejecting everything in sight!

SAYBut you know what? That reminds me of something.

ASKWho told the people at Babel to build that tower?

ASKAnd what had God already told Noah's family to do?

SAYNow watch what God does.

Genesis 11:7-9
7Go to, let us go down, and there confound their language, that they may not understand one another's speech.
8So the LORD scattered them abroad from thence upon the face of all the earth: and they left off to build the city.
9Therefore is the name of it called Babel; because the LORD did there confound the language of all the earth: and from thence did the LORD scatter them abroad upon the face of all the earth.

OPTIONALDid y'all catch verse 7? They said "let us build" (verse 4). God said "let us go down."

🍬 2. Did God knock their tower down?
No! The Bible never says the tower fell. Follow up: Did they run out of bricks? Then what happened? Their language was confused, and "they left off to build the city."

OPTIONALIf they ask what happened to it: the Bible doesn't say. It never says it fell, they just quit. Historians believe Babylon's big tower (the one in the picture) was in ruins almost 300 years later, when Alexander the Great, who conquered most of the world, decided to rebuild it. Strabo, a Greek history writer from around the time of Jesus, says just clearing the rubble was a job for 10,000 men for two months. Alexander died in Babylon in 323 BC, historians believe in Nebuchadnezzar's old palace, before it was ever rebuilt. Today, what historians believe is its footprint is still in the ground in Iraq. (History, not the Bible.)

Left: what historians believe is part of the base today, in Iraq, filled with water. Right: the old dig plan, the tower is the dark square. (Photo: Marjon Verburg, CC0. Plan: Robert Koldewey, public domain.)

ASKAnd what were they trying to avoid in the first place?

ANSWERBeing scattered.

ASKAnd what happened anyway?

ANSWERThey got scattered.

SAYIsn't that something? They worked so hard to avoid the very thing God told them to do.

SAYYou can have a good plan, good friends, and everybody working together. But before you start building anything, talk to God about it first. Make sure it's the direction He wants you to go.

Part 5. Pride, and asking God first 4 min

James 4:6
6But he giveth more grace. Wherefore he saith, God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble.

SAYPride isn't always bragging or thinking you're better than everybody. Sometimes pride sounds like: "I know what I'm doing." "I don't need advice." "I've got this figured out." "I don't need to ask God about this."

SAYBeing confident isn't the problem. Trusting my own judgment and ignoring God's direction is.

Proverbs 3:5-6
5Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding.
6In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.
🍬 3. Have you ever gotten so focused on doing something that you forgot to ask whether it was the right thing to do?
Anyone who shares gets candy. Don't push if nobody does.

SAYLast lesson, Noah built because God told him to. This lesson, the people at Babel built to make a name for themselves. Both had plans. Both were builders. Both worked toward a goal. But only one was following God's instructions.

Psalm 127:1
1Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it: except the LORD keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain.

Just because you're busy building something doesn't mean you're building what God told you to build.

Extra fun facts, if they ask

OPTIONAL"Babble" doesn't come from "Babel." Everybody thinks so, but word historians believe it just imitates baby talk.
