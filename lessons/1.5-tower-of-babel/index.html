Lesson 1.5: The Tower of Babel

Genesis 11:1-9 · Series 1: In the Beginning · Ages 14-17, about 30 kids · About 25-30 minutes · Big idea: Just because you're busy building something doesn't mean you're building what God told you to build.
Written by Andrew Young
Bring: 2 firm boards or tables (carpet) · 2 colors of plastic cups · 30-second timer · the printed cards (foreman cards, 2 rejection notices, APPROVED / REJECTED) · inspector's clipboard, pen, tape measure, fake nose and glasses · candy. Before class: test the cups on the boards, brief the inspector, agree when she does the rejection. During the opening: a few boys quietly set up the two stations.

1. Opening: Noah 5 min

Who remembers what we talked about last week?

What did Noah build? ...Right, the ark.

So did Noah just wake up one morning and go, "You know what? I want a giant boat"?

No. God told him to build it. And when they came off the ark, God gave them a job:

Genesis 9:1
1And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth.

Replenish the earth. Spread out and fill it up.

Now, a few generations later, we've got another group of people who decide to build something massive. But there's one pretty big difference. Noah built because God told him to. These people wanted to make a name for themselves.

Genesis 11:1-4
1And the whole earth was of one language, and of one speech.
2And it came to pass, as they journeyed from the east, that they found a plain in the land of Shinar; and they dwelt there.
3And they said one to another, Go to, let us make brick, and burn them throughly. And they had brick for stone, and slime had they for morter.
4And they said, Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.

Did y'all catch that? "Let us make us a name." And "lest we be scattered abroad."

🍬 1. What did God tell Noah's family to do? And what are these people trying to avoid doing?
Fill the earth (Genesis 9:1). They're trying NOT to be scattered. Let them make the connection themselves.
⭐ Fun fact: They really did stamp a name on the bricks

In the Bible, Babel and Babylon are the same word. Centuries later, King Nebuchadnezzar (the one from Daniel) rebuilt Babylon and stamped HIS NAME into the bricks. Archaeologists have dug up thousands of them, and museums have them today. Same city, same idea: "let us make us a name."

For me: the bricks are from long after the tower, so this is a connection, not something Genesis says. Nebuchadnezzar said it himself in Daniel 4:30: "Is not this great Babylon, that I have built... for the honour of my majesty?"

Life lesson: having a good plan doesn't automatically mean it's God's plan.

2. Before the game: they were united 6 min

Genesis 11:5-6
5And the LORD came down to see the city and the tower, which the children of men builded.
6And the LORD said, Behold, the people is one, and they have all one language; and this they begin to do: and now nothing will be restrained from them, which they have imagined to do.
⭐ Fun fact: They built up, God came down

They built a tower "whose top may reach unto heaven." And verse 5 says "the LORD came down to see" it. Their biggest thing ever, and God still had to come down to look at it.

If they want more: look at their words, "Go to, let us..." (verses 3 and 4). God answers with their own words in verse 7: "Go to, let us go down."

Look what God Himself says about them: "the people is one, and they have all one language." Everybody was working together. Everybody had the same goal. And it worked, they were good at it. But what they were working toward was the opposite of what God said to do.

Have y'all ever been with a group of friends where everybody agreed to do something stupid?

Life lesson: just because everybody agrees doesn't mean everybody is right.

They had one language, so they could coordinate everything. Bricks, slime, who's carrying what.

Don't read verses 7-9 yet.

So these people had the materials, the manpower, and a plan. But how much of their success depended on being able to understand each other?

Well... we're about to find out.

3. Babel Construction Company 10 min

  • Boys vs. girls, one station each, about 15 per team.
  • One foreman and two builders per team at a time. Everyone else waits in line.
  • Every 30 seconds the buzzer goes and all three rotate out. Five rotations = everyone gets a turn. Run more if there's time.
  • First 30 seconds: normal talking. After the first buzzer, every new foreman gets a secret card. Only the foreman reads it.
  • The inspector roams the whole time. She never stops the game.

The announcement (I read it out loud):

Welcome to Babel Construction Company! Each team will be constructing the tallest tower possible using the materials provided.

THE WINNER WILL BE DETERMINED BY THE FINAL HEIGHT OF THEIR STANDING TOWER.

Now, a few things you need to know. You'll have a foreman and two builders working at a time. Every 30 seconds, the buzzer will sound, and all three will be replaced. Each new foreman will receive a special communication instruction that must be followed at all times.

Our official building inspector will be conducting inspections throughout construction. She has the authority to issue rejection notices, require modifications, and demand immediate corrections to building-code violations. All rejection notices must be communicated by the foreman to the builders. Builders may not read rejection notices. At the conclusion, the inspector will conduct a final inspection and issue an official approval or rejection.

Any questions? ...Good. Because I probably wasn't going to answer them anyway.

LET THE CONSTRUCTION BEGIN!

Only height wins. The inspector's approvals, rejections and building codes do NOT count. Don't remind them until the end.

About halfway: the inspector fails BOTH towers and hands each foreman a rejection notice: five cups of the other team's color somewhere in rows 3-5. The foreman has to explain it under their card's rule, and passes the notice to the next foreman. The timer never stops.

When time is up: everybody stops. The inspector does her final inspection, APPROVED or REJECTED (just for show). Then I ask:

Okay. Which team actually won?

She measures. Tallest standing tower wins. (If the inspector's favorite LOST, she gets to be outraged: "EXCUSE ME?!" · "The rules said tallest tower wins." · "Well, that's a terrible system.")

4. Who authorized this? 2 min

Did I tell y'all how to win? ...What did I say? ...Build the tallest tower.

Then why were y'all so worried about making the inspector happy?

You got so busy following all these other instructions that you lost sight of the original one.

Inspector: "These towers are impressive, but I have one question... WHO AUTHORIZED THIS CONSTRUCTION?"

That's actually a very good question. Because when we left off in Genesis 11, the people were building something impressive. But who told them to build it?

5. Finish the story 6 min

Genesis 11:7-9
7Go to, let us go down, and there confound their language, that they may not understand one another's speech.
8So the LORD scattered them abroad from thence upon the face of all the earth: and they left off to build the city.
9Therefore is the name of it called Babel; because the LORD did there confound the language of all the earth: and from thence did the LORD scatter them abroad upon the face of all the earth.
🍬 2. Did God knock their tower down?
No! The Bible never says the tower fell. They didn't run out of bricks, and they didn't forget how to build. What changed? Their language. And "they left off to build the city."

They built that tower so they wouldn't be scattered. And what happened? They got scattered.

  • Genesis 9:1, God's command: fill the earth.
  • Genesis 11:4, man's plan: stay together and make a name for ourselves.
  • Genesis 11:8, God's answer: they were scattered.

Life lesson: you can be busy, talented, successful, and surrounded by people who agree with you, and still be heading in the wrong direction.

Have you ever been so focused on doing something that you forgot why you were doing it?

Do you ever stop and ask God whether what you're working toward is actually what He wants?

Proverbs 3:5-6
5Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding.
6In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.
For me: Genesis doesn't say they forgot God's command or that they didn't pray. Those are questions for US, not facts about them.

6. If there's time: Pentecost 3 min

At Babel, they all had one language, and they used it to make their own name great. At Pentecost, people from every nation heard, each in his own language...

Acts 2:4-11
4And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance.
5And there were dwelling at Jerusalem Jews, devout men, out of every nation under heaven.
6Now when this was noised abroad, the multitude came together, and were confounded, because that every man heard them speak in his own language.
7And they were all amazed and marvelled, saying one to another, Behold, are not all these which speak Galilaeans?
8And how hear we every man in our own tongue, wherein we were born?
9Parthians, and Medes, and Elamites, and the dwellers in Mesopotamia, and in Judaea, and Cappadocia, in Pontus, and Asia,
10Phrygia, and Pamphylia, in Egypt, and in the parts of Libya about Cyrene, and strangers of Rome, Jews and proselytes,
11Cretes and Arabians, we do hear them speak in our tongues the wonderful works of God.

..."the wonderful works of God." The difference isn't one language or many languages. It's whose name is being lifted up. (And look at verse 6: the crowd was "confounded," the same word as Babel. Only this time God was the one getting the attention.)

7. Closing 3 min

Last week, we learned about Noah. Noah built because God told him to.

This week, we learned about people who built because they wanted to make a name for themselves.

Both had plans. Both were builders. Both were working toward something. But only one was following God's instructions.

There's nothing wrong with having ambition, working hard, or wanting to accomplish something. But we need to make sure that what we're building is what God wants us to build.

🍬 3. What would you do? Your friends all agree on a plan for the weekend. It's fun, nobody thinks it's bad, but nobody has asked God either. What do you do?
EVERYONE who answers gets candy.

Just because you're busy building something doesn't mean you're building what God told you to build.
